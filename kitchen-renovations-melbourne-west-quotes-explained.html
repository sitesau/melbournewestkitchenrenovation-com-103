Preparing Quote Inputs and Understanding Materials

A truly useful measure-up goes beyond simply taking measurements. To ensure the designer has a clear understanding of your needs, prepare by considering how your current kitchen is used, identifying any functional issues with the existing layout, noting which appliances you intend to keep, and clarifying your primary renovation goals. Whether your aim is to enhance resale presentation, create a more functional space for daily family use, increase storage capacity, or undertake a complete rebuild, these details are vital. They help the designer distinguish between decisions related to the layout and those concerning the finishes.

When reviewing quotes, ask for them to clearly outline the scope of work, the total price, the agreed payment stages, and the process for handling any variations that may arise during the project. Projects typically commence with an itemised, fixed-scope quote, with each stage of the renovation agreed upon before it begins.

Regarding materials, it's important to be aware of current regulations. As of 1 July 2024, engineered stone containing 1% or more crystalline silica has been banned for manufacture, supply, processing, and installation across Australia. Current options discussed with homeowners include porcelain, sintered stone, natural stone, laminate, stainless steel, solid timber, and compliant low-silica alternatives. Material selection should be guided by both aesthetic appeal and practical use. Clarify how the benchtop will be templated, what cabinetry tolerances are assumed, how splashback and appliance interfaces will be managed, and when final material selections need to be locked in. For kitchen renovations in period homes, specifically inquire about how new cabinetry will integrate with older walls, floors, and openings without compromising the intended character.

Contracts, Timing, and Handover Paperwork

In Victoria, domestic building work valued over $10,000 requires a written major domestic building contract under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995. Additionally, domestic building insurance typically applies when the contract value exceeds a certain threshold, which in mid-2026 was commonly cited as more than $16,000. It is advisable to consult current regulator guidance before signing any contract, as thresholds and insurance names can change, especially with reforms to Home Warranty Insurance anticipated from 1 July 2026.

The published renovation process generally follows four stages: a free measure-up, design and quoting, the build and installation phase, and finally, handover. Handover signifies the completion of a fully functional kitchen, accompanied by all necessary warranty and compliance paperwork provided in writing. It is beneficial to inquire about this documentation early in the process, particularly when plumbing, electrical work, appliance installations, and benchtops are all being coordinated under a single renovation schedule. This ensures all parties are aware of their responsibilities and the expected deliverables.

  1. Define the Scope. Decide whether your kitchen needs a simple makeover, a full rebuild, custom storage solutions, or a period-sensitive redesign. This initial decision sets the direction for the entire project.
  2. Book a Measure-Up. Use the measure-up visit to clearly explain how you use your current kitchen, what aspects are not working, and which appliances or services might need relocation. This provides essential context for the designer.
  3. Review Drawings. Before cabinetry is manufactured, confirm the proposed layout, material selections, and lighting plan. This step ensures the design aligns with your vision and functional requirements.
  4. Check the Written Quote. Ensure the quote itemises the scope of work, specifies a fixed price, details payment stages, lists any required permits, outlines trade responsibilities, and explains the process for handling variations.
  5. Confirm Handover Documents. Ask about the specific warranty and compliance paperwork that will be provided upon project completion. This documentation is crucial for future reference and peace of mind.
